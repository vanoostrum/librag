Do Kahneman and Taleb agree on whether experts can make good long-term forecasts?

Both authors doubt expert forecasts in fields with low regularity. Kahneman describes Philip Tetlock's study of political experts, who predicted barely better than chance.1 He separates this from fields with stable patterns and fast feedback, where expert intuition can be valid.2 Taleb goes further: rare, high-impact events make long-range forecasting in social and economic fields close to useless.3

SynthesisThey agree on the diagnosis but differ in scope. Kahneman names the conditions under which expertise works. Taleb treats forecasting in his domains as suspect altogether.

General knowledge, not from your libraryThe two discussed each other's work in public. Your books hold no passage on that.
